# The Five Marquee Passages

These five passages get the full tap-a-word interlinear treatment (design doc, pillar 4).
Selected 2026-08-14 by Claude at the builder's delegation ("choose the passages yourself
using best judgement"). Each passage is attached to one stop; `marquee_passage` in that
stop's frontmatter is the machine-readable source of truth.

## Why these five

1. **The proem (1.1–5)** — the most famous opening in Western literature, and the word
   πολύτροπον ("of many turns") is the single richest interlinear target in the poem:
   untranslatable, argued over for 2,300 years, and the anchor of an etymology card.
   Placed at Troy so the voyage opens the way the poem does.
2. **The Nobody trick (9.366–370)** — Οὖτις ("Nobody") and the μή τις / μῆτις
   ("no one" / "cunning") pun are the entire justification for the interlinear feature:
   a joke that literally cannot survive translation. Includes Polyphemus's chilling
   guest-gift reply — "Nobody I will eat last."
3. **The Sirens' song (12.184–191)** — the only place we hear the Sirens' actual words;
   flattery engineered for its listener ("great glory of the Achaeans"). Also the
   designated audio set piece if the v1.1 sound layer lands.
4. **Scylla (12.85–92)** — the deep-stop template for M3, the flagship etymology chain
   (Σκύλλα → σκύλαξ "puppy"? → Scilla, Calabria), and the poem's most unsettling sound
   image: a monster with a newborn puppy's voice.
5. **Calypso refused (5.215–224)** — Odysseus, offered immortality, chooses a mortal
   wife and a rocky island: the thesis statement of nostos and the emotional core of
   the whole site. Paired with Ogygia, the NOW toggle's showcase stop (Gozo).

**Runners-up considered**: the bed test (23.183–204 — lives in Ithaca's endgame bridge,
not a full stop, so ineligible); Argos the dog (17.291–327 — same); rosy-fingered dawn
formula (better served by an etymology/formula card than an interlinear).
