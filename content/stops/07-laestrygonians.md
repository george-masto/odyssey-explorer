tidbits:
  - text: "Eleven of twelve ships are destroyed at their moorings with every man aboard: on this atlas's ledger, 477 of the 522 who left Troy die in this one harbor — against six taken by Scylla and six eaten in the Cyclops's cave. The poem's largest massacre takes barely fifteen lines, and not one of the dead is named."
    source: "Od. 10.118–132; twelve ships: Il. 2.637; a ship's company of ~46 implied at Od. 10.203–209"
  - text: "Alone of the captains, Odysseus does not enter the inviting harbor — he ties up to a rock outside the mouth. When the ambush springs he cuts his own cable with his sword and shouts his rowers out to sea: the one act of pure sailor's caution that leaves anyone alive to tell the story."
    source: "Od. 10.95–96, 10.126–132"
  - text: "The harbor is drawn with a pilot-book's precision: unbroken cliff on both sides, twin headlands pinching a narrow entrance, water inside never ruffled by a wave. Victor Bérard matched the description point for point to Bonifacio's cliff-slot inlet at Corsica's southern tip, and the identification — unprovable, irresistible — has anchored the Laestrygonians there ever since."
    source: "Od. 10.87–94; Victor Bérard, Les Phéniciens et l'Odyssée (1902–1903)"
  - text: "One line made ancient scientists stare: at Telepylus the paths of night and day lie close together, so a sleepless herdsman could earn double wages — one wage herding cattle, one pasturing white sheep. Crates of Mallus and other early geographers read high-latitude summer nights into it, a midnight sun inside the Odyssey — though it may only be a folk-memory of a land where the herding never stops."
    source: "Od. 10.82–86; ancient geographic speculation, cf. Strabo 1.2.13"
