tidbits:
  - text: "Circe's un-pigging is an upgrade: restored, the men come out younger, taller, and more beautiful than they went in. Then everyone simply stays. It takes a full year of feasting before the crew pulls Odysseus aside — δαιμόνιε, 'strange man, remember your fatherland' — and the man who out-schemed the Cyclops has to be begged to go home."
    source: "Od. 10.395–396, 10.467–474"
  - text: "Moly, the antidote Hermes pulls out of the earth — black at the root, milk-white in flower, hard for mortals to dig though the gods can do all things — is the first named magic herb in Western literature. Identifying it became a scholarly sport millennia before pharmacology: Theophrastus, botany's founder, already discusses a real-world moly, and moderns have nominated everything from snowdrop to wild rue."
    source: "Od. 10.302–306; Theophrastus, Historia plantarum 9.15.7"
  - text: "The cruelest detail is the quietest: the transformed men have a pig's head, voice, bristles, and body, 'but their minds remained unchanged even as before'. The horror of Aeaea is not becoming an animal — it is staying yourself inside one, weeping in the sty."
    source: "Od. 10.239–240"
  - text: "Strabo describes Circaeum as a mountain that wears the look of an island, rising between sea and marsh, and reports an altar of Circe and a cup displayed as Odysseus's own. The geography still performs the trick: from a boat offshore, Monte Circeo detaches from the flat Pontine coast exactly like an island — which is all the identification ever needed."
    source: "Strabo, Geography 5.3.6"
