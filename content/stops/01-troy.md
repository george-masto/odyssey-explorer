tidbits:
  - text: "Nine superimposed cities lie in the mound at Hisarlik. The candidate for Homer's Troy has moved with each generation of diggers — Schliemann bet on Troy II, a thousand years too early; the current favorite is Troy VIIa, a crowded town destroyed by fire around 1180 BC. It remains a hedge, not a fact."
    source: "Hisarlik excavations, Schliemann (1870s) to Korfmann (1988–2005); Troy VIIa identification debated"
  - text: "The man of the first line is kept anonymous: Odysseus is not named until line 21. The poet repeats the trick inside the story — his hero keeps arriving nameless (Nobody, a beggar) and having to win his identity back."
    source: "Od. 1.1, 1.21"
  - text: "The Odyssey never narrates the sack of Troy in its own voice. Odysseus, incognito at a Phaeacian feast, requests the song of the wooden horse from the bard Demodocus — then weeps at his own story 'as a woman weeps' over a husband killed in a falling city. The sacker mourns like the sacked."
    source: "Od. 8.499–531"
  - text: "The Iliad's Catalogue of Ships gives Odysseus twelve ships, and this atlas's crew arithmetic starts there: twelve crews, roughly 600 men. None of them will see Ithaca — the proem admits as much at once, blaming 'their own blind folly'."
    source: "Il. 2.637; Od. 1.6–9"
