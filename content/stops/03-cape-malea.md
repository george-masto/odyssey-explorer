tidbits:
  - text: "For ancient geographers this cape is where the poem leaves the map. Eratosthenes drew the line at Malea — everything beyond it he declared unlocatable, quipping that you will trace the route of Odysseus's wanderings on the day you find the cobbler who stitched the bag of the winds."
    source: "Strabo 1.2.15, reporting Eratosthenes"
  - text: "Malea is the Odyssey's recurring trapdoor for homecoming fleets: Menelaus's squadron is split here and hurled to Crete and Egypt, Agamemnon is swept past it on the way home to his murder, and Odysseus loses ten years. Three homecomings founder on one headland."
    source: "Od. 3.286–300, 4.514–520, 9.80–81"
  - text: "Fear of rounding Malea made other people rich: Strabo explains the wealth of Corinth by merchants preferring to land their cargoes at the Isthmus and haul them across, rather than sail around the cape."
    source: "Strabo 8.6.20"
  - text: "Nine days before the winds is Homer's favorite span of lostness — nine years at Troy, nine days of plague in the Iliad, and nine days adrift again after Thrinacia. Taken literally here, it is several hundred sea-miles: far enough to carry the identifications all the way to Tunisia."
    source: "Od. 9.82–83; cf. Od. 12.447, Il. 1.53"
