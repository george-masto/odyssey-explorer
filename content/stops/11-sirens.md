tidbits:
  - text: "The survival protocol is Circe's, not Odysseus's: knead honey-sweet wax into the crew's ears, lash the captain upright in the mast-step — and, the crucial clause, if he begs to be freed, add more rope. It is literature's first pre-commitment device; behavioral economists still call self-binding arrangements 'Ulysses contracts'."
    source: "Od. 12.47–54"
  - text: "Circe undercuts the song before it is sung: the Sirens sit in a flowering meadow beside 'a great heap of bones of rotting men, and the skins shrivel around them'. Homer prices the seduction and the body count in a single sentence."
    source: "Od. 12.45–46"
  - text: "Homer never says what the Sirens are — no wings, no fish-tails, no faces, only a voice and a meadow. He does reveal their number: the duals νωιτέρην (12.185) and Σειρήνοιιν (12.52) mean exactly two. The bird-body is archaic vase-painters' invention, the mermaid a medieval one."
    source: "Od. 12.52, 12.185"
  - text: "Tradition anchors the Sirens at Li Galli, three islets off Positano known since antiquity as the Sirenusae. Choreographer Léonide Massine bought them in 1922; Rudolf Nureyev bought them in 1988 and they remained his private refuge until his death in 1993."
    source: "Strabo 1.2.12; modern history"
