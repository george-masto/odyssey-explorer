tidbits:
  - text: "Zeus's bolt is a negotiated price. Told by the nymph Lampetie that his cattle are dead, Helios threatens to quit the sky and go shine among the dead below — so Zeus promises to smash the offenders' ship with his bright bolt in mid-sea. The storm is not weather; it is an invoice being settled."
    source: "Od. 12.374–388"
  - text: "Blown back to Charybdis on the night wind, Odysseus leaps for the great fig tree above the whirlpool and clings to it 'like a bat' — he can neither plant his feet nor climb, for the roots are far below and the branches out of reach overhead, so he simply hangs, waiting for the sea to give his mast and keel back."
    source: "Od. 12.426–437"
  - text: "Homer times the whirlpool by a magistrate's working day: the spars resurface at the hour when a judge who has settled the day's quarrels of young men rises from the assembly and goes home to supper. Odysseus lets go, plunges down beside the beams, and paddles out with his hands."
    source: "Od. 12.439–444"
  - text: "Then nine days adrift on the lashed spars; on the tenth night the gods beach him on Ogygia, Calypso's island. The loneliest stretch of the ten-year journey takes the poem three lines."
    source: "Od. 12.447–450"
