I know full well of myself that wise Penelope is meaner to look upon than thou in comeliness and in stature, for she is a mortal, while thou art immortal and ageless. But even so I wish and long day by day to reach my home, and to see the day of my return. And if again some god shall smite me on the wine-dark sea, I will endure it, having in my breast a heart that endures affliction. tidbits:
  - text: "Even the messenger god turns sightseer: Hermes skims the waves like a seabird, then stops before the cave — alder, poplar and cypress full of nesting owls and falcons, a garden vine heavy with clusters, four springs running four ways, violet and parsley meadows, cedar and juniper smoke drifting off the hearth fire. Homer grants it twenty lines, the loveliest set-piece landscape in either epic, and says outright that even a deathless god would gaze and be glad."
    source: "Od. 5.55–74"
  - text: "Ordered to give him up, Calypso rounds on the gods: 'cruel are ye, jealous beyond all others' — Dawn's mortal lover Orion and Demeter's Iasion were struck down for being loved by goddesses, while gods take mortal women freely. Read now it lands as a proto-feminist protest against the double standard (a modern framing — the poem gives her the grievance, not the victory)."
    source: "Od. 5.118–129"
  - text: "The proportions of the wanderings are strange: one year with Circe, days or weeks everywhere else — and seven years, most of the decade at sea, hidden on Ogygia. Odysseus gives Queen Arete the number himself: seven years weeping on the shore, wetting the immortal clothes Calypso gave him."
    source: "Od. 7.259–261; cf. 10.467–469"
  - text: "Freed at last, he gets no ship: Calypso hands him a double-edged bronze axe with an olive-wood haft. He fells twenty trees, drills, joins, decks and rigs a raft, finishing on the fourth day — the most detailed picture of a craftsman at work in Homer, and a preview of the carpenter who once built a bedroom around a living olive tree."
    source: "Od. 5.234–262"

now_today: "Tradition parks Ogygia on Gozo, Malta's second island: above Ramla Bay — the island's famous red-gold beach — a cliff grotto is signposted as Calypso's Cave. The cave itself is closed as unstable, but a viewing platform beside it looks down the whole bay, and the beach below is the best swim on the island. Ferry from Malta to Mġarr, then a short drive through Xagħra; the tourist board happily bills Gozo as the isle of Calypso."
