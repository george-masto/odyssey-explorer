  en: "From Ilios the wind bore me and brought me to the Cicones, to Ismarus. There I sacked the city and slew the men; and from the city we took their wives and great store of treasure, and divided them among us, that so far as lay in me no man might go defrauded of an equal share. Then verily I gave command that we should flee with swift foot, but the others in their great folly did not hearken. But there much wine was drunk, and many sheep they slew by the shore, and sleek kine of shambling gait."

tidbits:
  - text: "The loss is reported as a per-ship deduction: six benched comrades from each of the twelve ships — 72 men gone at the very first landfall. Homer's bookkeeping is exact and appalling, and it is what lets an atlas like this one keep a running crew count."
    source: "Od. 9.60–61"
  - text: "Maron's wine is the poem's Chekhov's gun. Odysseus protects the priest of Apollo during the sack; the thank-you gift is wine so strong it was cut with twenty measures of water. Homer hangs that wineskin on the wall here so it can go off in the Cyclops's cave, two stops later."
    source: "Od. 9.196–211, 9.345–362"
  - text: "This is not random piracy: the Cicones fought at Troy as allies of the Trojans, so the raid is the war's final engagement — and the counterattack, by inland Cicones 'skilled at fighting from chariots', is the war refusing to stay finished."
    source: "Il. 2.846; Od. 9.47–49"
  - text: "The whole disaster runs on the poem's signature failure mode: Odysseus orders a swift withdrawal, and the crew — 'in their great folly' (μέγα νήπιοι) — keeps drinking on the beach. The proem has already given the same verdict on all of them: they perished by their own recklessness."
    source: "Od. 9.43–44; Od. 1.7"
