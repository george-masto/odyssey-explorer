tidbits:
  - text: "The dead cannot speak until they drink from the trench of sacrificial blood, and Tiresias spells out the protocol: whichever ghost Odysseus allows near the blood will speak truth to him; whichever he refuses will withdraw. Memory itself, here, is a substance in short supply."
    source: "Od. 11.146–149"
  - text: "Three times Odysseus lunges to embrace his mother's ghost; three times she flits through his hands — like a shadow or a dream. Anticlea explains the physics: the sinews no longer hold flesh and bone, the pyre consumed them, and the soul flies off like a dream."
    source: "Od. 11.204–222"
  - text: "Tiresias assigns the strangest errand in epic: after killing the suitors, carry a well-fitted oar inland until someone mistakes it for a winnowing-shovel, plant it, and sacrifice to Poseidon — then death will come off the sea, gentle, in sleek old age. The phrase ἐυῆρες ἐρετμόν is the very formula for the oar planted on Elpenor's grave-mound at 12.15."
    source: "Od. 11.119–137"
  - text: "Achilles' retort — better a hired serf for a landless man than king of all the dead — lands like the Odyssey's rebuttal of the Iliad's glory-ethic, put in the mouth of the man who chose the short glorious life. Readers have staged the two poems' duel over these four lines since antiquity."
    source: "Od. 11.488–491"

now_today: "The poem's underworld reception has a real address in Epirus: the Acheron. Its springs near Glyki are now a summer canyon — rafting, riverside tavernas, wading tourists in glacial water — while downstream at Mesopotamo the excavated Necromanteion of Ephyra claims the oracle of the dead, its underground crypt open to visitors (some archaeologists counter that the 'oracle' was a fortified Hellenistic farmhouse). The rival claim lies west of Naples, at Lake Avernus."
