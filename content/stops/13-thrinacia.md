tidbits:
  - text: "Odysseus extracts a 'mighty oath' that no one will touch cattle or sheep. It survives a month of trapped calm and dwindling stores — until, with Odysseus asleep inland, Eurylochus makes the argument that undoes them: all deaths are hateful, but starving is the worst of them; better the sea's one great gulp. They even vow Helios a temple on Ithaca as compensation before they kill."
    source: "Od. 12.298–304, 12.339–365"
  - text: "Helios's ultimatum to Olympus is cosmic blackmail: pay fit atonement for the cattle, or 'I will go down to Hades and shine among the dead.' A sun-strike — light withdrawn from the living world — averted only when Zeus promises to handle the executions himself."
    source: "Od. 12.377–388"
  - text: "The price is paid at sea, exactly as promised: one thunderbolt, the ship spun and filled with sulphur, the crew bobbing 'like sea-crows' around the hull until the god takes away their homecoming. Odysseus — the only man who never ate — survives astride a lashed keel and mast."
    source: "Od. 12.415–425"
  - text: "How can Odysseus narrate a conversation held on Olympus while he slept on Thrinacia? He audits his own sources mid-story: Calypso told him, and she had it from Hermes. A footnote inside the epic — the first-person narrator briefly showing his seams."
    source: "Od. 12.389–390"

now_today: "Taormina's Greek theatre — Hellenistic, rebuilt in brick by the Romans — is cut into the hillside above this very coast, and its broken scaena deliberately frames the two protagonists of the episode's landscape: the Ionian below, where the ship burned, and Etna smoking above. Herds grazing under an active volcano make the Sun's untouchable cattle feel less like allegory here; the theatre now fills for summer festivals, the coast for beach season."
